# Stage 3 · Creative engine

**The process is fixed. The plot is not.** This file gives you a bar, a method, techniques and traps. It is not a template library.

## The bar: what "creative" means here

A technically clean ad with a predictable idea fails. A real test run produced "a bored lady sips the latte → the rain stops and the orchid blooms". It was well made and on-brand, and the reviewer still rejected it: *"you drink it and the weather clears — that's not creative."* That is the category's default story with new props.

An idea is worth making only if it passes all five:

1. **Swap test.** Replace the product with a competitor's drink. If the story still works, it's not an idea about *this* product.
2. **One-sentence surprise.** Tell the logline to a stranger. They should smile or say "oh!". "X drinks Y and feels better" fails.
3. **Hero image.** There is one frame people would screenshot or share. Name it.
4. **Payoff.** The last act lands something: a twist, a reveal, a gag, a callback to the opening. "And then everything is nice" is not a payoff.
5. **Product as engine, stage or star, not medicine.** The product causes something surprising, *is* the world, or is the character. It does not just cure a bad mood.

## Category clichés (don't use as the core idea)

These are what every drink ad does. Use one only as a deliberate setup you then subvert.

- sip → the sun comes out / the rain stops / flowers bloom / color returns to a grey world
- sip → tired person is energised / stressed person relaxes / frown becomes a smile
- slow-motion pour, splash, ice cubes dropping, ingredients raining into the cup
- the product floating among clouds, fruit or sparkles
- "escape to paradise" (beach, sky) after the first sip
- a crowd or friends cheering with cups

The worked example in `example-line-art-grape.md` (tired student → sip → grape balloons) is a **quality** reference, not a creativity reference. Its core is the cliché "sip → escape". Aim higher.

## Step 1 — inventory what only this product has

From `brand_profile` and `product_spec`, list 10+ specific things:
- **packaging and logo**: characters or motifs printed on the cup, the logo's figure, the cup's shape, the sleeve, the lid
- **name**: its imagery, a pun in the market's language, the literal meaning
- **ingredients and their physics**: pearls bounce and roll, foam is a snowfield, syrup stripes drip, crushed ice crackles, nuts are boulders
- **ritual**: how people actually drink it (stir first? eat the topping with a spoon? shake?)
- **sensory**: aroma, temperature, the sound of the straw
- **brand culture and place**: its city, art references, history, its mascot's personality

## Step 2 — diverge: at least 10 raw ideas

Generate fast and don't judge yet. Use **at least five different techniques** from the table. Each idea is one line.

| Technique | How it works | Generic illustration |
|---|---|---|
| Packaging comes alive | a figure or pattern printed on the cup or logo steps out and acts | the cup's printed figure climbs down its own pattern like a trellis |
| Personify an attribute | the aroma, the chill or the chewiness becomes a character with a goal | the cold of an iced tea is a tiny penguin looking for a home |
| Scale shift / reveal | a big world turns out to be the cup (or the cup is a planet) | an epic snowy summit is the foam cap; pull back to reveal the cup |
| Match-cut rhyme | a shape becomes another shape (pearl → planet, foam → cloud sea, stripe → tiger) | brown-sugar stripes peel off and prowl as a tiger cub |
| Setup → payoff gag | misdirection or the rule of three, with a punchline | three attempts to steal a sip, each foiled, until the fourth succeeds in an unexpected way |
| Role reversal / POV | the ingredient's point of view, or the drink "drinks" back | a pearl's nervous journey up the straw like a rollercoaster |
| Cultural remix | the brand's culture × an unexpected genre | a classical painting staged as a heist, a sports commentary, a nature documentary |
| Literal name / pun | the product's name taken literally, in the local language | the drink's name acted out word by word |
| Impossible physics with rules | one invented law of physics, applied consistently | whenever someone sips, gravity flips for a second |
| Callback loop | the ending mirrors the opening with a twist | the opening's problem returns at the end and becomes the gag |

## Step 3 — converge: shortlist 3, pitch, score

For each of the 3 strongest: a **logline** (one sentence), the **hero image**, the **payoff**, and the act outline. Then score:

| Criterion | Weight | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| Surprise | ×3 | Would a stranger smile or say "oh!" at the logline? |
| Only this product | ×2 | Does it fail the swap test with a competitor? (It should.) |
| Payoff | ×2 | Does the end land a twist, reveal, gag or callback? |
| Brand fit | ×1 | Style, tone and culture feel like this brand? |
| Product clarity | ×1 | Is the product shown correctly and appetisingly, ending on the hero shot? |

**Gates (pass/fail, not points):** causality (`Step 5`) and feasibility (one main action per 5 s; no finger work, crowds, readable text or fast juggling). If a high-scoring idea fails feasibility, **simplify its execution** before choosing a weaker idea. Fewer characters, a static cup, the camera doing the work and a slower beat are all ways to do that. Don't fall back to a cliché because it's easier.

Record all three in `concept.json` with scores, the hero image, the payoff, and why two lost. If the winner fails any item of **the bar**, go back to Step 2.

## Step 4 — structure and expand

| Structure | Shape | Length | Segments (5 s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Two-act** | setup → turn/punchline (a single gag, reveal or transformation) | 10–15 s | 2–3 |
| **Three-act** | setup → escalation → twist/payoff, then the hero shot | 20–25 s | 4–5 |

The product hero shot is the final beat unless the user says otherwise. Ideally it is part of the payoff (the reveal *is* the cup, or the character returns to its place on the cup), not a separate "and here's the product" card.

Expand into acts → 5 s segments → acting beats (`storyboard-format.md`). Each segment has one main action, at least one exaggerated cartoon action (stretch, squash, bounce), one expression change, and one camera beat. Write beats as **acting**:
- ✗ "the character picks up the cup and drinks"
- ✓ "flops face-down with a squashy bounce → slowly lifts its head, peeks sideways → its arm stretches across the frame like a noodle, grabs the cup and snaps back → sips with puffed cheeks, eyes squeezed shut → a happy full-body wiggle"

## Step 4b — design the bridge into the hero shot (mandatory)

The jump from the story world to the product shot is the moment people remember, so it can't be a plain camera pull-back or a cross-dissolve. In testing, a pull-back + dissolve ("the night sky was the cup's print") was rejected as dull. These were the endings people liked:
- grape balloons pop → grapes rain down → they land as real grapes beside the cup
- a final qin chord shakes the jasmine-blossom stars loose → they rain down → they land as real jasmine → the real cup drops in and lands with a thud in the middle of the ring

A good bridge is a **physical, causal hand-off**: something from the story *travels* into the product shot and changes form on arrival. Ideally it's the product's real ingredient, so the bridge also tells you what's in the cup.

| Bridge type | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Rain / fall | a story action knocks something loose → it falls, the camera follows → it lands as the real ingredient → the cup drops or settles into place among it |
| Pour / flow | something in the story flows (a river, ink, a sound wave, moonlight) → the flow carries the camera → it pours into or becomes the real drink |
| Throw / catch | a character throws or kicks an object toward the camera → it flies in an arc → it is caught by, or lands as, the real product |
| Match-cut on shape | the last story shape is matched by the product's shape (a full moon → the lid seen from above; a round pearl → a real pearl rolling into frame) |
| Wipe by motion | a big story motion (a wave, a gust of petals, a galloping horse) sweeps across the frame and leaves the hero shot behind it |
| Step out of the world | a character walks, jumps or slides out of the drawn world onto the real table, next to the cup, then freezes into the cup's print or logo |

Rules:
- The bridge has a **cause in the story** (a chord, a pop, a kick), not a cut.
- What arrives in the hero shot must **exist in K_end**: if jasmine lands, K_end has a ring of jasmine. Build K_end to match (`compose_hero.py --bg-image … --base-y …` puts the product exactly where the ingredients are).
- The product may **fall, slide or drop into place**, keeping its exact shape (no squash or stretch on the cup). It may not morph, grow or shrink relative to its surroundings.
- Put the bridge in the last segment's beats (`0–1 s` cause, `1–3.4 s` travel and transformation, `3.4–4.2 s` arrival and impact, `4.2–5 s` settle) and give it a sound (rustle, patter, a soft thud, a chime).

Record it in `concept.json` as `bridge`.

## Step 5 — causality pass (mandatory)

For every segment, ask: *after this happens, what must each object on screen do next?*

- Transformations are complete: a popped balloon disappears; a melted thing stays melted.
- Consequences follow causes: no balloons → the character falls → it lands on something.
- Anything magic has a visible cause and a visible effect, and follows the idea's own rules every time.
- Props nobody touched stay exactly where they were.

Worked failure: "the balloons burst into grapes" while the character kept floating. Nothing was holding it up any more, and every viewer noticed. Fixed: the balloons pop one by one and vanish, the strings go limp, the character drops onto a cloud.

## Traps

- Any cliché above as the core idea; a plot that passes the swap test.
- Reusing the example's plot for a different brand.
- Picking the safest idea because it is easiest to generate (simplify the bold idea instead).
- Two or more main actions in one 5 s segment.
- Small text, signs or writing as story elements.
- Many characters or crowds, fast multi-object juggling.
- Fruit that isn't recognisable: describe it by shape and growth, not by name.
- Off-style background effects (full-screen speed lines, tinted clouds in a white-background style).
- Stories that need the product itself to morph or be squeezed. The cup stays still and dignified. Its printed figures, toppings and aroma may come alive.
